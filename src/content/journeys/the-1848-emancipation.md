## The Road to Freedom

By the 1840s, the institution of slavery in the Danish West Indies was under increasing pressure — both from abolitionist movements in Europe and from the enslaved people themselves, who had long resisted through rebellion, marronage, and daily acts of defiance.

In 1847, the Danish King Frederick VII issued a decree that all enslaved people would be freed — but not until **1859**, twelve years in the future. The enslaved population of St. Croix refused to wait.

## July 3, 1848

On the morning of July 3rd, thousands of enslaved people gathered in the town of Frederiksted, demanding immediate and unconditional freedom. At the center of this mass mobilization was **General Buddhoe** (John Gottlob), who is credited with organizing the march and presenting the ultimatum to the colonial authorities.

The crowd was enormous, determined, and unified. Governor Peter von Scholten, faced with the overwhelming force of the people's will, declared:

> *"Now you are free. You are hereby emancipated."*

With those words, slavery in the Danish West Indies came to an end — not on the colonizer's timeline, but on the people's.

## The Aftermath

Emancipation did not bring true freedom. The colonial government quickly implemented restrictive labor codes that kept formerly enslaved people bound to plantations under contract. This injustice would eventually lead to the Fireburn of 1878.

But July 3rd, 1848 remains a pivotal moment — a testament to the power of collective action and the courage of people who refused to accept oppression for a single day longer than they had to.

Today, July 3rd is celebrated as **Emancipation Day** in the U.S. Virgin Islands.
